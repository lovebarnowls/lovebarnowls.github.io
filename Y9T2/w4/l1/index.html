# Week 4, Lesson 1 — Relief Teacher Guide ## "Predicting valid input" — RetroPixel Arcade This guide assumes no technical background. You do not need to understand Flask, Python, or how the website works — just follow the steps below to check whether a student is on the right track, or to point them in the right direction if they're stuck. **Important:** every student's flag is unique (based on their name), so this guide cannot give you the exact flag text to look for. Instead, for every task it tells you **exactly what to type into the address bar**, which is the same for every student. If a student types that in correctly, the right page will appear on its own — you don't need to know what the flag says, just that a flag *appears*. --- ## Before you start - Students begin by typing their name into a pop-up box. This is what makes their flags personal to them — if two students swap flags, the flags won't work for each other. - **Do not refresh the browser tab once a student has started.** There is no save function — refreshing resets their score and flag count back to zero (though they can quickly re-find and re-submit the same flags, since they'll be looking in the same places). - The address bar inside the lab (near the top of the screen, next to three small dots) is **not** the real browser address bar — it's part of the activity itself. Typing into it and pressing Enter navigates within the lab. - Every correct answer in this lesson ends in **`.html`**. If a student is confident they've found the right idea but nothing happens, the first thing to check is whether they've left `.html` off the end. - **New this lesson:** students meet `?parameter=value` web addresses for the first time (used in B4, A1, and H1). `app.py` now opens with a short plain-English explanation of this near the top, and two of the earlier flag pages (found via B2 and B3) drop in a "side note" showing a worked example before students need to build one themselves. If a student seems completely lost on B4 or A1 specifically, check they've actually read those side notes rather than skipping straight past them. --- ## Task-by-task: what to type, and what should happen | Task | Points | Type this into the address bar | What should appear | |---|---|---|---| | **B1** | 50 | `/vip-lounge.html` | A page with a purple background and a boxed flag. | | **B2** | 50 | `/machines/galaxian7.html` | Same style — purple background, boxed flag. | | **B3** | 50 | `/machines/PINBOT.html` | **Must be typed in capital letters exactly like this.** Lowercase (`pinbot.html`) will not work — that's the point of this task. | | **B4** | 50 | `/vote.html?machine=tempest` | A "Vote Recorded — Tempest" page with a boxed flag. The word after `machine=` must be exactly `tempest`. | | **A1** | 10 | `/debug.html?user=maharai_dev` | A "Debug Panel" page with a boxed flag. The word after `user=` must be exactly `maharai_dev` — this comes from a comment hidden in the source of `staff-notes.html` (see below for how to view it). | | **A2** | 10 | `/machines/04-04.html` | A "Cabinet 04-04" page with a boxed flag — but first the student needs to click **Edit** on `machines/index.html` and delete a commented-out line to reveal the link (see below). | | **H1** | 1 | `/highscore.html?year=1980` (any year from 1978 to 1983 also works) | **The flag will NOT appear on the page itself.** The student must click the **"Headers" tab**, just above the Source panel, to see it. | | **F1** | 0 (not scored) | No typing needed — just click the task card. | Opens a separate tool for building their own practice site with Copilot. Not part of the scored lesson. | --- ## If a student is stuck: step-by-step for each task ### B1 — VIP Lounge 1. Have them click task **B1** in the task list. This opens `app.py` in the Source panel automatically. 2. Point them to the first block of code, starting with `@app.route('/vip-lounge.html')`. 3. Tell them: whatever text is inside the quote marks after `@app.route`, that's what you type into the address bar. 4. Clear the address bar, type `/vip-lounge.html`, press Enter. ### B2 and B3 — Machine pages 1. Still looking at `app.py`, find the block starting `@app.route('/machines/')`. 2. Explain: this one route handles many different pages, depending on what comes after `/machines/` in the address. Underneath, a list of `if` / `elif` lines shows which exact words work. 3. For **B2**: the line `if machine_id == 'galaxian7.html':` means typing `/machines/galaxian7.html` works. 4. For **B3**: the line `elif machine_id == 'PINBOT.html':` — point out this one is in capital letters, unlike the others. Typing has to match capital letters exactly, the same way a password would. ### B4 — Vote 1. Have them open task **B4** — this takes them to `leaderboard.html`, which shows a dropdown with five machine names (pacman, tempest, defender, joust, centipede) but explains the dropdown doesn't actually do anything yet. 2. Tell them: the real answer is in `app.py`, not on this page — click the Explorer panel on the left and open `app.py`. 3. Find the block `@app.route('/vote.html')`. It checks whether `machine` equals `'tempest'`. 4. Type into the address bar: `/vote.html?machine=tempest` 5. If a student asks what the `?machine=tempest` part means: explain that everything after the `?` is like filling in a blank on a form, built right into the web address itself. `machine=tempest` means "the answer to the question 'machine' is tempest." ### A1 — Debug 1. Have them open task **A1** — this takes them to `staff-notes.html`. 2. The page looks mostly empty. Tell them to look at the **Source** panel on the left (not the preview on the right) — somewhere in the HTML there's a line starting with ``. That's a comment, invisible on the actual page, but visible in the Source panel. 3. It mentions a specific username. 4. Back in `app.py`, find `@app.route('/debug.html')` — it checks a `user` value. 5. Type into the address bar: `/debug.html?user=maharai_dev` ### A2 — Cabinet pattern (uses the Edit button) 1. Have them open task **A2** — this takes them to `machines/index.html`, which lists three cabinets: 04-01, 04-02, 04-03. 2. This page has an **Edit** button above the Source panel (look for it next to the lock icon). Have the student click it. 3. Somewhere in the text they'll see a line wrapped in `` — that's an HTML comment, meaning it exists but is switched off. 4. Have them delete just those two markers (``), leaving the line itself in place. 5. Watch the preview on the right — a fourth cabinet link should now appear. 6. Clicking that link (or typing `/machines/04-04.html` directly) shows the flag. - **Why it changed:** this used to just be "guess the next number in the pattern," which is the same trick as a Week 2 task. This version still uses the same cabinet numbering, but now the actual discovery is through editing — which most students will already have seen in Week 3, so it's a nice refresher rather than a brand new skill. ### H1 — Response headers 1. Open task **H1** — this takes them back to `app.py`. 2. Find `@app.route('/highscore.html')`. It checks whether a `year` value is between 1978 and 1983. 3. Type into the address bar: `/highscore.html?year=1980` 4. The page will say "You found the golden age window" — **this is not the flag**. Tell the student to click the **"Headers"** tab (next to "Source", just above the text box on the left). The flag is on a line starting with `X-Arcade-Debug`. 5. This is the point of the task: not every answer shows up on the page itself. ### F1 — Formative (not scored) - This is a separate activity, not a flag hunt. Clicking it opens a tool with locked instructions at the top and two boxes for the student to fill in (their chosen theme, and what security issues they want to plant). There's a "Copy full prompt for Copilot" button — once clicked, the student pastes the result into Copilot to start building their own practice site. - Nothing to check here in terms of flags — this task exists purely to get students started on their own build, which continues into Lesson 2. --- ## Troubleshooting **"404 — no such file" appears on the right, or nothing changes at all.** Almost always one of these three things: - `.html` is missing from the end. - A letter is the wrong case (check B3 and A1 especially — both require exact capitalisation somewhere). - A typo in the path itself. Have the student re-read the relevant line in `app.py` character by character rather than guessing from memory. **Student typed the flag but got "WRONG."** Check for extra spaces, or that they copied the *whole* flag including `faith{` and the closing `}`. Partial copies won't match. **Student typed the right thing and got "DECOY — this was a decoy flag."** This is expected for one specific fake string hidden in a `# TODO` comment near the bottom of `app.py`. It's meant to look real but isn't linked to anything. No cause for concern — just explain that not everything flag-shaped in the source is a real flag, and have them keep looking at the real routes above it. **The "Copy" button (receipts box, or the Copilot prompt tool) doesn't do anything when clicked.** This can happen if the lab is being tested by opening the file directly rather than through the real deployed website — copying to the clipboard sometimes needs a proper web address to work. If this happens, students can just manually select and copy the text instead. **A student refreshed the page and lost their progress.** There's no way to recover it — they'll need to re-enter their name and re-find their flags. Since the answers are in the same places, this is usually quick the second time. **A student asks why their flag looks different from a friend's for "the same" task.** Every flag includes the student's own name in how it's generated, so flags are never shared between students, even for the same task. This is intentional — copying a friend's flag will not work.
